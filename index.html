Wind Shifts

The objective of this simulation is to develop the student's ability to recognize and respond to natural wind shifts while sailing upwind. You will learn to identify headers and lifts by observing the wind indicator, the boat's heading, and the behavior of the sail.

The goal is not to react to every change in the wind. The goal is to recognize the change, understand what it means for your course, and make the correct sailing decision.

The Upwind Rule

An ILCA cannot sail directly into the wind, so you must zig-zag upwind. For this simulation, your target upwind angle is 45° to the wind. When the wind direction changes, your boat's relationship to the windward mark changes immediately.

What is a LIFT? (The Good Shift)

A lift occurs when the wind rotates away from your bow. Your boat can then sail higher while maintaining its upwind course.

  • Instrument clue: The ILCA wind vane moves outward toward the side of the dial.
  • Boat reaction: The boat can continue sailing efficiently, but the windward mark is now at a higher angle relative to the boat.
  • Sailing decision: HOLD & HEAD UP! Use HEAD UP to bring the bow closer to the wind and take advantage of the lift.

What is a HEADER? (The Bad Shift)

A header occurs when the wind rotates toward your bow. Your current upwind course becomes less favorable, and the windward mark moves farther to one side of your bow.

  • Instrument clue: The ILCA wind vane moves toward the red 45° BOW zone.
  • Boat reaction: The boat begins to lose its effective upwind course as the wind comes closer to the bow.
  • Sailing decision: RECOGNIZE THE HEADER. When the header makes your current tack unfavorable, TACK to take advantage of the lift on the opposite tack.

Wind moves away from your bow Drifts outward HEAD UP
Wind moves toward your bow Enters BOW zone Consider TACK

The Big Idea

Don't sail the boat by the compass alone. Sail by the wind. When the wind changes, recognize the shift, understand what it is doing to your course, and make the right sailing decision.
